# Spaced Repetition: The Science of Long-Term Retention

Have you ever spent hours studying or learning a new skill, only to forget almost everything a week later? If so, you are experiencing the **Forgetting Curve**—a natural, mathematical representation of how information slips from our minds over time.

Fortunately, there is a scientifically proven antidote: **Spaced Repetition**.

In this guide, we will break down what spaced repetition is, how it beats the forgetting curve, and how **Rewise** automates this system to make your learning permanent.

---

## What is the Forgetting Curve?

In the late 19th century, German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus conducted pioneering experiments on memory. He discovered that without active reinforcement, memory retention drops off exponentially:

* **Immediately after learning:** 100% retention
* **Within 24 hours:** Retention drops to roughly 50-80%
* **Within a week:** Retention can plummet to less than 20%

Ebbinghaus concluded that memory decay is predictable, but it can be interrupted.

---

## Beats the Curve: The Spaced Repetition Solution

Spaced repetition is a learning technique where review sessions are scheduled at increasing intervals. Each time you review a topic just before you are about to forget it, you strengthen the neural pathway. This resets the forgetting curve, making the rate of decay much shallower.

Here is what happens when you introduce spaced reviews:

1. **First Review (1 day later):** Reinforces the initial connection.
2. **Second Review (3 days later):** Memory is consolidated, requiring a longer time to decay.
3. **Third Review (7 days later):** Turns short-term understanding into conceptual storage.
4. **Fourth Review (15 days later):** Locks in the details.
5. **Fifth Review (30 days later):** Achieves near-permanent retention.

At this point, the knowledge is moved into your **long-term memory bank**, requiring very little effort to recall months or even years down the line.
